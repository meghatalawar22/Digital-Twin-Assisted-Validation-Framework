# Digital Twin-Assisted Validation Framework for Autonomous Vehicle Safety and Behavior Testing

A MATLAB-based virtual vehicle simulation and Arduino Mega 2560
prototype for lane-following, obstacle detection, stopping, warning, and
automatic restart. The project compares recorded physical-vehicle
telemetry against expected outputs generated by MATLAB controller logic
through **offline telemetry replay**.

> **Scope clarification:** This is a MATLAB base-graphics simulation
> (not a Simulink block-model). The Twin does not connect to the Arduino
> live. The hardware sends telemetry to MATLAB, and a separate
> validation script replays the recorded input samples through the Twin
> controller logic.

## Project Overview

The framework contains three parts:

1.  **MATLAB Digital Twin** --- an animated single-lane oval-track
    virtual vehicle with simulated IR and ultrasonic sensor values,
    Arduino-matched decision logic, and scenario selection.
2.  **Physical Arduino vehicle** --- a four-wheel DC-motor vehicle
    controlled by an Arduino Mega 2560, L298N motor driver, two IR
    sensors, HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor, buzzer, SG90 servo, and HC-05
    Bluetooth module.
3.  **Telemetry logging and validation** --- MATLAB captures serial
    telemetry from the Arduino, stores the data in a `.mat` file, and
    compares each recorded sample against expected controller outputs
    from the Twin logic.

## Features

-   Single-lane oval-track visualization in MATLAB
-   Arduino-matched lane-following decision logic
-   Infrared lane sensing (left and right)
-   Ultrasonic obstacle-distance measurement
-   Obstacle stop and latched-stop behavior
-   Warning buzzer with distance-dependent beeping
-   Automatic restart after obstacle clearance
-   Consecutive-clear-reading condition before restart
-   Kick-start sequence to help overcome motor starting friction
-   HC-05 Bluetooth telemetry over the Mega's hardware serial port
-   Offline Twin-versus-hardware validation
-   Validation of mode, PWM, stop latch, clear counter, and buzzer state
-   Timing, stopping-distance, restart-delay, plots, and
    confusion-matrix reporting

### HC-05 Serial Wiring

The Arduino sketch uses `Serial1` on the Mega 2560:

-   HC-05 **TXD → Mega RX1 (D19)**
-   Mega TX1 (D18) → HC-05 **RXD through a voltage divider**
-   HC-05 VCC → 5 V (module board supply pin as applicable)
-   HC-05 GND → common GND

Use a voltage divider on the Mega TX1 line before the HC-05 RXD input
(the wiring diagram uses 1 kΩ and 2 kΩ). Connect grounds together.

### Power Notes

-   Use the motor supply for the L298N motor-power input.
-   Use the separate Arduino supply path through the buck converter to
    Mega VIN/DC jack as shown in the wiring diagram.
-   **All grounds must be common:** Arduino, L298N, sensors, Bluetooth
    module, and power supplies.
-   Remove the L298N ENA/ENB jumpers when controlling enable pins using
    Mega D11 and D6.
-   Verify the actual supply voltage and polarity before powering the
    circuit.

## Controller Parameters

The Arduino and MATLAB Twin use matching controller parameters:

  Parameter                         Value Meaning
  ---------------------------- ---------- ---------------------------------
  Normal forward PWM                   60 Straight movement
  Turn PWM                             90 Lane correction
  Kick-start PWM                      130 Restart burst
  Kick-start duration              150 ms Restart burst duration
  Obstacle stop threshold           20 cm Stop at or below this distance
  Warning threshold                 30 cm Buzzer warning zone
  Resume clearance threshold     \> 35 cm Distance considered clear
  Consecutive clear readings            6 Required before restart
  Servo angle                         90° Ultrasonic sensor faces forward
  Twin time step                    20 ms Simulation step

## Lane-Following Logic

The IR sensors use the following truth table in the controller:

      Left IR    Right IR Action
  ----------- ----------- ------------
    0 (white)   0 (white) Forward
    1 (black)   0 (white) Turn right
    0 (white)   1 (black) Turn left
    1 (black)   1 (black) Stop

The controller stops the vehicle if the ultrasonic distance is at or
below 20 cm. When stopped, it requires six consecutive readings above 35
cm before attempting restart. The kick-start routine applies a PWM burst
for 150 ms and checks the obstacle distance during the burst; if an
obstacle is still within the stop threshold, the kick is aborted and the
vehicle remains stopped.

## Buzzer Behavior

-   At or below the stop threshold (20 cm): continuous buzzer
-   Between the stop and warning thresholds (20--30 cm): beeping
    interval changes with distance
-   Beyond the warning threshold (30 cm): buzzer off

The warning-zone buzzer comparison in validation is timing-tolerant
because the buzzer is driven by periodic timing and telemetry sampling.

## MATLAB Digital Twin

The Twin is a MATLAB animated simulation of a virtual vehicle on a
predefined single-lane oval track. It uses simulated IR states and
obstacle-distance readings; it does **not** read live Arduino sensors.

### Twin Scenarios in the Script

The supplied Twin code supports:

1.  **No obstacle / lane following** --- vehicle follows the lane
    without an obstacle.
2.  **Obstacle removed after 14 seconds** --- obstacle detection,
    stopping, clearance observation, and restart behavior.
3.  **Persistent obstacle** --- vehicle stops and remains latched while
    the obstacle persists.
4.  **IR truth-table demonstration** --- cycles through all four IR
    combinations to demonstrate controller responses.

The research paper's reported validation scenarios are Scenarios 1--3;
Scenario 4 is a code-level sensor truth-table demonstration and is not
included in the paper's reported scenario results.

The Twin approximates vehicle movement for visualization. It is not a
full physical dynamics model.

## Running the Arduino Vehicle

1.  Wire the components using the pin mapping and wiring diagram.
2.  Check that all power connections and common grounds are correct.
3.  Confirm that the HC-05 is configured for 115200 baud.
4.  Open the Arduino sketch in Arduino IDE.
5.  Select **Arduino Mega 2560** and the correct COM port.
6.  Upload the firmware.
7.  Place the vehicle on the test lane and verify IR sensor states.
8.  Test motor direction, obstacle stopping, buzzer, and restart
    behavior in a controlled area.

## Running the MATLAB Digital Twin

1.  Open the Twin MATLAB script.
2.  Run the script.
3.  Select scenario `1`, `2`, `3`, or `4` when prompted.
4.  Observe the virtual vehicle, simulated sensor values, controller
    states, and validation plots.

The Twin is simulation-only and does not communicate with the physical
Arduino.

## Recording Hardware Telemetry

The telemetry logger uses the Bluetooth COM port (the example uses
`COM15`) and baud rate `115200`.

1.  Pair the HC-05 with the computer.
2.  Identify the Bluetooth serial COM port assigned by Windows.
3.  Update `port` in the MATLAB logging script if needed.
4.  Set the test duration and scenario number.
5.  Run the MATLAB logger.
6.  Run the physical vehicle test during the logging window.
7.  The logger saves the table as `hw_run_<scenario>.mat`.

### Telemetry Record Format

Arduino sends CSV records beginning with `T,`:

``` text
T,seq,ms,L,R,dist,stIn,clrIn,mode,outA,outB,buzzer,stOut,clrOut
```

  Field            Description
  ---------------- -----------------------------------------------
  `seq`            Sequence counter
  `ms`             Arduino elapsed time in milliseconds
  `L`, `R`         Left and right IR sensor readings
  `dist`           Ultrasonic distance in centimeters
  `stIn`           Stop-latch state at the beginning of the loop
  `clrIn`          Clear counter at the beginning of the loop
  `mode`           Controller mode code
  `outA`, `outB`   Signed motor PWM commands
  `buzzer`         Buzzer output state
  `stOut`          Stop-latch state after controller processing
  `clrOut`         Clear counter after controller processing

Mode codes:

    Code Meaning
  ------ -----------------------
       0 Forward
       1 Turn right
       2 Turn left
       3 Both-black stop
       4 Obstacle stop
       5 Latched stop
       6 Kick-start successful
       7 Kick-start aborted

## Twin--Hardware Validation

Validation is performed offline after hardware telemetry is collected.

1.  Load the recorded `.mat` telemetry table.
2.  For each record, run the Twin controller function using the logged
    IR, distance, stop-latch input, and clear-counter input.
3.  Generate expected Twin controller mode, PWM, stop-latch,
    clear-counter, and kick state.
4.  Compare expected outputs against the hardware record.
5.  Calculate individual match percentages and combined all-checks
    matching.
6.  Report mismatches and produce diagnostic plots.

### Validation Checks

-   Controller mode match
-   PWM match for both motor commands
-   Stop-latch state match
-   Clear-counter match
-   Buzzer consistency (with timing tolerance in the warning zone)
-   Overall record match across all checks

### Reported Outputs

The validation script reports: - Total records compared - Mode match
percentage - PWM match percentage - Stop-latch match percentage -
Clear-counter match percentage - Buzzer match percentage - Percentage of
records passing all checks - Confusion matrix - Mismatch samples -
Median hardware loop period - Obstacle stop event count and stopping
distance statistics - Hardware restart delay, when available - Twin
restart-delay estimate based on the configured clear-reading count and
simulation step

## Experimental Results (Paper Dataset)

The reported paper validation runs used three scenarios:

  Scenario    Description                                   Records
  ----------- ---------------------------------------- ------------
  1           Lane following without obstacle                 4,647
  2           Obstacle removal and automatic restart          4,858
  3           Persistent obstacle                             5,164
  **Total**                                              **14,669**

For the collected dataset, the paper reports 100% matching for the
evaluated controller-level checks across all three scenarios. This
result indicates consistency between recorded hardware controller
outputs and the expected outputs generated by the Twin logic for these
data---not general proof of safety or complete physical equivalence.

Other recorded measurements include: - Hardware median loop period:
**18.0 ms** - Scenario 2 stopping distance: **18.8 ± 1.9 cm** - Scenario
3 stopping distance: **20.0 ± 0.0 cm** - Scenario 2 average restart
delay: **8.68 s**

## Limitations

-   The MATLAB Twin uses simulated sensor inputs, not live sensor
    streams.
-   Telemetry is compared through offline replay; there is no real-time
    Twin--hardware synchronization.
-   Vehicle motion is approximate and is not based on a complete
    physical dynamics model.
-   Motor response, mechanical variations, sensor noise, surface
    conditions, and timing differences can affect the physical vehicle.
-   Validation conclusions are limited to the recorded tests, scenarios,
    and controller checks.

## Safety

-   Keep the vehicle on a controlled test track.
-   Secure the vehicle before performing motor wiring or electrical
    changes.
-   Disconnect power before rewiring.
-   Check the motor-driver and battery voltage ratings.
-   Avoid short circuits and reverse polarity.
-   Ensure the vehicle has room to stop during testing.
